Title: The $100,000 Gift That Disappears: Why the Sandwich Generation is Building Suites, Not Buying Mortgages

The Hook:
If you're in your 50s and simultaneously supporting aging parents and adult children, you've likely felt the "Double Squeeze." In 2026, the traditional playbook—gifting a down payment or paying for private care—is a clinical wealth leak. The new math says: build equity, don't burn it.

The "Sting" (The Math):

The Gift Evaporation: A $100,000 "gift" to help your child buy a $1M home disappears into mortgage interest at 5.5%. Over 25 years, that gift costs your child $155,000 in interest alone—and you see zero return.

The Care Burn: With 290-day waitlists for public Long-Term Care beds in BC, families are paying $11,000/month for private "bridge" care. That's $132,000 per year—burning through a decade of retirement savings in under 12 months.

The Missed Opportunity: The Canada Secondary Suite Loan Program (CSSLP) offers up to $80,000 at 2% fixed interest for 15 years. Instead of gifting money that vanishes, you can build a suite that generates $2,800/month in rental income or saves $4,000/month by housing a parent at home.

The Story:
Maria, 54, was about to give her son $120,000 for a condo down payment. At the same time, her mother's care home was costing $10,500/month. We showed her the math: that $120,000 could build a 2-bedroom suite in her Langley home. Her son moved into the suite rent-free (saving him $2,400/month), and six months later, her mother moved into the main floor bedroom, eliminating the care home cost entirely. Maria didn't "spend" $120,000—she deployed it. The suite added $180,000 to her home's value, and she reclaimed $14,900/month in family cash flow.
